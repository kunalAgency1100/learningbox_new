The everyday work of leadership

Where better management starts

Good intentions can still leave a team guessing. Give people a clear picture of the work, a reliable way to raise concerns, and feedback they can act on. These habits help managers and younger employees work through differences before they become recurring friction.

fact_check

Make expectations visible

Define the outcome, deadline, owner, and what “done” looks like. Ask the employee to explain the plan back in their own words so you can catch gaps early.

forum

Give useful feedback

Describe a specific action and its effect, then agree on the next step. Address issues close to when they happen and leave room for the employee’s perspective.

calendar_month

Keep a regular check-in

Use one-to-ones to review priorities, blockers, workload, and development. Close each conversation with a clear decision or follow-up.

workspace_premium

Explain growth paths

Show what stronger performance looks like in the current role, which skills matter for the next role, and what evidence would support a promotion discussion.

balance

Set healthy boundaries

Agree on response times, escalation channels, and what counts as urgent. Discuss workload openly and reprioritize when new work arrives.

diversity_3

Build trust through follow-through

Invite questions without making assumptions about age or motivation. When you promise an answer or action, return with an update even if the decision is still pending.

Put it into practice

Three conversations worth getting right

Use these prompts as a starting point, then adapt them to the person and situation.

When a deadline slips

Find the cause and reset the plan before assigning blame.

“What got in the way, what can we still deliver, and when should we check in again?”

When feedback is difficult

Name the observable behavior and invite a response.

“In yesterday’s review, the handoff was missing two details. What happened, and how can we make the next one complete?”

When someone wants to grow

Turn an open-ended ambition into a development plan.

“Which role are you working toward, and what project could help you demonstrate the next skill?”

A simple starting plan

A 30-day manager reset

Small, repeated changes are easier to sustain than a one-off workshop. Use this sequence to establish a baseline, improve conversations, and see what still needs attention.

  1. Week 1 · Listen. Ask each person what helps them do their best work, where instructions are unclear, and how they prefer to receive feedback.
  2. Week 2 · Clarify. Agree on priorities, owners, decision rights, deadlines, and communication norms.
  3. Week 3 · Practice. Hold one structured feedback conversation and one development conversation with each direct report.
  4. Week 4 · Review. Ask what has become clearer, what still causes delays, and which team agreements need updating.
